01 Story one of three

The warm water is sinking

Twenty-five years of readings from one mooring in the Rockall Trough show the heat moving down, not just in.

+0.41 °Cat 800 metres in 2025, against the 1995 to 2004 average5

Our RT-2 mooring has hung thermometers at fixed depths since 2001. At 200 metres the water swings with every winter storm. At 800 metres it barely moves, which is why a steady climb there matters more.

2025 was the warmest year in the deep record. The surface layer warmed less6: the heat is going down, where cold-water corals live.

The dip in 2014 to 2016 is the cold patch that spread across the North Atlantic in those winters. The deep layer recovered within three years.

02 Story two of three

Living coral on 17 of 41 survey stations

In May the Halcyon mapped a field of seamounts on the Hatton Bank, then sent the camera down four lines across it.7

  • 604m

    The summit of Halcyon Knoll, the shallowest point we mapped. Nobody had charted it closer than a 1 km grid before.

  • 640 to 1,760m

    The depth band where every living reef sat. Below it, the camera found only dead coral rubble.8

  • 312km²

    The area we have put forward for protection from bottom trawling, drawn around the 17 reef stations.9

03 Story three of three

Slower ships, quieter water

For 22 weeks, ships in the Minch agreed to sail at 10 knots. Our hydrophone listened all year.10

−5.4 dBmedian noise during the trial, 105.2 dB against 110.6 dB in the other 30 weeks

Decibels are logarithmic. A drop of 5.4 dB means the water carried about a third of the sound energy, every hour of the day, for five months.11

Other weeks100
Trial weeks29

Harbour porpoise clicks, logged on the same hydrophone, were heard on 41% more days during the trial than in the same weeks of 2024.

Where the money went

£7.14m in, £6.82m out

Most of our income comes from people giving monthly. Most of our spending is ship time, because the deep sea can only be studied from a ship.

79pof every £1 spent went on research, data and learning12

The £0.32m left over went into the ship fund, which pays for Halcyon’s mid-life refit in 2028.
