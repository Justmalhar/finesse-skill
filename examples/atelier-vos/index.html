01

Opbouw Vierhavenstraat

Merwe-Vierhavens, completed June 2025, for Woonstichting Maaskant

The 1951 warehouse drawings said the frame could take one more floor. We drilled fourteen cores from its columns before drawing a line, found 40 percent more strength, and put two storeys of cross-laminated timber on top: light enough to need no new piles.

How we work

  1. Measure the old building first

    Most of our work sits on something that is already there. Before we draw, we core the concrete, open a floor and dig beside a pile. The opbouw on Vierhavenstraat exists because its 1951 drawings were wrong in our favour.

  2. Timber where it carries, brick where it weathers

    Cross-laminated timber for floors and walls, glulam for long spans, and old Waalformaat brick at street level where bikes and bins knock into it. We do not clad concrete in wood to make it look warmer.

  3. Joints you can undo

    Panels are screwed and bolted, never glued on site. Every building leaves with a list of its parts, so a floor can come out in 2080 and go into somebody else’s house.

  4. Neighbours at the table

    On the first Thursday of each month the studio stays open until nine. Anyone living next to one of our sites can sit down, look at the drawings and argue with us about them.

Three people, one drawing table

We take on four buildings a year. That keeps it small enough for one of us to draw every joint at full size before it goes to the factory in Heerde.
